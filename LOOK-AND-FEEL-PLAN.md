# TAG Gridiron look and feel plan (draft 2026-11-08)

Goal: use everything learned from D:\MaddenRef to make TAG Gridiron look and feel like a top tier football game, while every shipped asset stays ours or licensed to us.

## 1. Ground rule: MaddenRef is a measuring stick, not content

MaddenRef holds Madden NFL 19's own art: 12,182 decoded player clips, bodies, 185 helmet and gear meshes, heads, coaches, the 49ers 2018 uniform set and stadium pieces. Shipping any of it, or anything derived from it, would build TAG Gridiron out of EA's art. That rules out:
- MaddenRef meshes, textures and materials built from its presets in game content.
- Madden clips, including the retargeted `A_<clip>_UEFN` copies, driving the Athlete.
- Tracing or fitting our meshes to theirs, sampling their textures, or keying our animation from their curves.

What MaddenRef is good for, and what this plan uses it for:
- **Side by side comparison.** The same camera and pose in the reference project and in our game, so we can see where ours falls short.
- **Aggregate numbers.** Proportions, timing distributions and material value ranges, used as targets next to real world references.
- **Coverage checklist.** The clip taxonomy tells us which situations a football game has to show and how much variety it takes.

Reasons, briefly:
- The README and `docs/ANIMATION_SPEC.md` promise original, freely licensed assets.
- The install's provenance is unresolved (`codex.cfg` and `OrangeEmu64.dll`).
- TAG is a public brand, so builds labelled private will not stay private.

**Real NFL teams and players.** These need NFL and NFLPA licences, and published Madden ratings are EA's compiled data. Recommendation: ship the fictional league. Rate players from open data and our own measurables model. Add a local roster editor and import, so a player can rename teams on their own machine. Many independent sports games take this route.

- **Pipeline (`Tools/Anim`, Blender 5.2 headless).**
  - `mocap_retarget.py`: a retargeter from the pack skeleton to the UEFN rig. The pack rest pose is calibrated to the rig (each mapped bone turned so it points at its next joint the way the rig's does), then every frame's world rotations transfer through those offsets; the hips translate scaled by the hip-height ratio (0.897). Fingers come across (the ball sits in the hand), the ik_* helpers copy the hands and feet. The frame of reference is the pack's `AITrajectory` (its local axes are X forward, Y up, Z right, measured on the clips): `inplace` strips its position and heading every frame (the sim moves and turns the actor), `inplace_pos` keeps the body's turns (spins), `anchor` strips only the first frame (falls, dives, get-ups carry their travel, as the keyed ones did). Mirroring reflects about the actor's X = 0 plane with per-bone axis flips. A torso fix turns the whole upper body rigidly so the spine-base-to-head direction matches the source (the two spines are shaped differently; without it a bent stance sat the head 10 cm low). Output: the same actor frame as the keyed clips (facing -Y, left +X, metres, 30 fps) and the same `A_TAG_<clip>.fbx` + `.json` (heights, planted-foot slide, plus the right hand's and right foot's peak-speed frames).

No more players appearing on their marks.

Against these: every throw's plant was 0.10 s before release and is now 0.20 (release and follow-through moved two frames; `THROW_RELEASE`, `ThrowKeyedStart` follow).

  - **The umpire** (`TickUmpire`): a sixth official who works from the offensive backfield (9 yd behind the ball, off the quarterback's line), jogs to the dead ball at the whistle, carries it in his hand to the next spot (`Game::ballOn`, `ballY`), sets it on the turf nose downfield and returns to his position; off the field for kicks. The ball is drawn in his hand and then on the new spot until the next play's sim takes over.

Ours: the receiver at 9.4 m/s runs 4.82 steps/s with a 1.95 m step (the proportional reference at 9.4 m/s is ~4.6 steps/s, 2.04 m: cadence 4 % high, step 4 % short); the corner breaking at 8.5 m/s runs 4.28 steps/s, 1.98 m.   - **Acceleration against NFL Scouting Combine splits** (`check_timing.py`): the sim's acceleration law (vmax 8.6 + (speed - 50) × 0.049 yd/s, tau 1 - (accel - 50) × 0.0078 s, dv/dt = vmax/tau × max(0.12, 1 - v/vmax)), integrated from a standing start, puts a 90-speed / 85-accel receiver at 1.59 / 2.58 / 4.47 s over 10 / 20 / 40 yd and an average one (86 / 82) at 1.63 / 2.63 / 4.56, against the Combine's 1.55 (10 yd, 2026 receivers), ~2.59 (20 yd, drafted players) and 4.52 (40 yd, 848 receivers since 2003; 4.44 in 2026). Every start, lean and stride on the field follows this curve.
  - **Sprint cadence, step and body height, repeated runs.** The gait log is noisy run to run (motion matching picks different clips): at the 1.3× play-rate cap the receiver at 9.4 m/s measured 4.8, 5.3, 6.0 and 5.8 steps/s (steps 1.95-1.57 m); at 1.1× 5.1 and 5.5 steps/s (1.85, 1.70 m). Hips while sprinting 0.85-0.89 m and head 1.40-1.46 m against the sprint bands of 0.90-0.99 and 1.32-1.54; the stride warp's hip drop was eased (22 → 10 cm per unit of stretch) to keep the hips up.

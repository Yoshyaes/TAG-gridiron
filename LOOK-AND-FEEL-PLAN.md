# TAG Gridiron look and feel plan (draft 2026-11-08)

Goal: use everything learned from D:\MaddenRef to make TAG Gridiron look and feel like a top tier football game, while every shipped asset stays ours or licensed to us.

## 1. Ground rule: MaddenRef is a measuring stick, not content

MaddenRef holds Madden NFL 19's own art: 12,182 decoded player clips, bodies, 185 helmet and gear meshes, heads, coaches, the 49ers 2018 uniform set and stadium pieces. Shipping any of it, or anything derived from it, would build TAG Gridiron out of EA's art. That rules out:
- MaddenRef meshes, textures and materials built from its presets in game content.
- Madden clips, including the retargeted `A_<clip>_UEFN` copies, driving the Athlete.
- Tracing or fitting our meshes to theirs, sampling their textures, or keying our animation from their curves.

What MaddenRef is good for, and what this plan uses it for:
- **Side by side comparison.** The same camera and pose in the reference project and in our game, so we can see where ours falls short.
- **Aggregate numbers.** Proportions, timing distributions and material value ranges, used as targets next to real world references.
- **Coverage checklist.** The clip taxonomy tells us which situations a football game has to show and how much variety it takes.

Reasons, briefly:
- The README and `docs/ANIMATION_SPEC.md` promise original, freely licensed assets.
- The install's provenance is unresolved (`codex.cfg` and `OrangeEmu64.dll`).
- TAG is a public brand, so builds labelled private will not stay private.

**Real NFL teams and players.** These need NFL and NFLPA licences, and published Madden ratings are EA's compiled data. Recommendation: ship the fictional league. Rate players from open data and our own measurables model. Add a local roster editor and import, so a player can rename teams on their own machine. Many independent sports games take this route.

## Status (2026-11-19): fumbles and interceptions from the captures, a football that bounces like one, bodies that take a hit

Goal of the night: proper fumble and interception animations, true ball physics and body physics, the game as close to the mocap as it can be.

- **The football is a rigid body** (`Sim/BallPhysics.h`). A prolate spheroid the size, weight and inertia of an NFL ball, with an orientation, a spin and drag, meeting the turf at whatever point of its surface is lowest (restitution 0.48, friction 0.55). A ball landing on its nose kicks off sideways or end over end; one on its belly skips and rolls; it comes to rest lying on its side. Nothing about a bounce is rolled at random. Loose balls (fumbles, unfielded kicks), tipped and batted passes (they tumble off their spiral) and dead incomplete passes all fly on it, and the renderer draws its orientation. A sim test drops 60 balls: all come to rest on their belly, none through the turf, and the bounces go every way (resultant 0.85 or less).
- **Fumbles.** The sim says how the ball came out (`strip` by the second man in a pile, `hit` at the tackle, `sack`) and pops it from the carrier with his momentum and the blow's direction. When the tackle that fumbles is played, it is one of the pack's two-man strip captures (`Tools/Art/pack_turnovers.py` measured 61; the 33 where the ball comes out play). The ball stays in the carrying arm the capture punches until the punch (0-1.1 s after the contact), then eases onto the sim's loose ball. The men nearest it go for it: at most two in the air at once, the nearest of each side, each diving at where the ball will be when his hands get there (a copy of the rigid ball flown ahead). A man running with the ball sitting up scoops it. A miss knocks it on. Recoveries play a dive capture timed to the touch, the nearest men pile on, and after the whistle the man who came up with it holds it up (the pack's fumble-recovery celebrations) while two teammates point the way. In 120 test plays at 60x the fumble rate: 2.3 dives a fumble, 2 of 144 dives land off the ball. The fumble rate was refit for the new recoveries: fumbles lost 0.48-0.49 a team-game over two seeds (NFL 0.45).
- **Interceptions, breakups and drops are decided before the ball arrives** (`PlaySim::preRollCatch`), the way a sports game picks its two-player interaction ahead of the contact: 0.4 s out, with the arrival's own odds on predicted positions, corrected so the outcomes match the old roll at the ball (two seeds: completion 62.0-62.3 % against 62.6 %, interceptions 0.80-0.82 a team-game). An interception then plays one of the pack's 51 two-man interception captures (defender and receiver in sync, chosen by where the receiver is, the defender's speed and the ball's height); a breakup is the named defender's swat; a drop is the receiver's own drop capture.
- **Bodies take a hit.** Two men meeting at speed share the collision's velocity change by mass (`AMatchDirector::DetectContacts`), and their trunk and head answer it on damped springs (`FTagRetargetProxy::ApplyImpact`, 2.2 Hz and 3 Hz): a 5 m/s collision tips the trunk ~7 degrees and snaps the head further, then it settles. 2-44 contacts a play. `-TagNoImpact` for A/B.

A physical DualSense was not connected for this run.
  - **Play art on the call screen (2026-11-10).** Every card on the play-call screen carries the play drawn the way the field draws it: the strokes are built once in the sim's yards (`Game/PlayArtStrokes.h`, `BuildArtPicture`) and both `ATagPlayArt` (the turf) and `SPlayDiagram` (a Slate leaf widget, `UI/PlayDiagram.h`) draw them. Cards grew to 270 x 236 with the diagram above the name. `-TagCallShot`: a user game that screenshots the offensive and then the defensive call screen (`tag_call_off.png`, `tag_call_def.png`).
- **Motion closer to real football (2026-11-10).** Goal: every animation as close to the real game as the keyed clips and the GASP capture allow. Measured against broadcast footage and coaching technique, the biggest gaps were ones the sim and the layering created, not the clips:
  - **Facing.** The sim set every moving player's facing to his velocity, so the quarterback dropped back with his back to the defense, pass blockers turned round as they kick-slid, and defenders in coverage never backpedalled (motion matching's backpedal and shuffle clips were never reached; `faceoff` averaged 3°). `PlaySim::move` now keeps the quarterback facing downfield through his drop, pass blockers facing the rush, and coverage defenders (zone, man, spy) facing the ball while they retreat under 5.5 yd/s before the throw; a defender turning and running deep faces where he runs. Facing is the renderer's only: the 100-game calibration (seed 7) is byte-identical to HEAD's, 21/21 sim tests.
  - **The quarterback.** Keyed drops (`Tools/Anim/clips_more5.py`): shotgun three step (1.8 m in 0.65 s, 2.75 m/s) and under-center five step (open the hip, crossover, plant and hitch; 6 m in 1.5 s, 4 m/s), played at the sim's drop speed (rate 0.7-1.5) while he backs out with the ball; then `QB_Pocket_Loop` (on the toes, ball at the chest, eyes scanning) in place of the standing idle, built on the set throw's first pose so the throw cuts in from it. Throws: the plant now sinks the hips (pelvis 0.90 → 0.81 m), the back heel comes up at release and the toe drags through the follow-through; release and follow-through hand targets brought inside the arm's reach (IK miss 16 → 5 cm).
  - **Defenders in space** break down (`Def_Breakdown_Loop`: hips sunk, chest up, arms out, 4 choppy steps/s) within 3.2 m of the carrier when under 2.3 m/s (linemen excepted).
  - **Body physics layer** (`TagRetargetAnimInstance`): the body leans from the ankles into its measured acceleration (tan a = a/g, 0.38×, at most 12° on top of what the capture's starts and plants carry), pelvis rotating about the soles with the legs taking the lean through IK; and planted feet are pinned to the turf on the ball of the foot (pin on touchdown, release past 36 cm or when the clip lifts the foot, 0.12 s ease-out) so in-place keyed clips and rate-capped locomotion stop skating. `-TagNoLean`, `-TagNoFootLock` for A/B. `AAthlete::SlideStats` measures planted-foot travel (animation vs shown); on the `-TagMoveTest` course the shown slide is 25-45 % below the animation's on every route (sprint + cut 745 → 551 cm, back curve 829 → 566, DB backpedal + break 1539 → 1232, lineman 1493 → 1154); the rest is the speed mismatch a pin can't hide (release blends) and the toe rolling through the stance. Cost: a full auto-watch game runs 45.3 fps with the layer and 45.6 without.
  - **Between plays** everyone jogs back to the next formation from where the last play left him (`StartPresnap` → `PlaySim::walkFrom`, 4.6 yd/s when there is ground to cover; the snap waits for the formation to set, up to 6 s; men over 32 yd away take their spot). No more players appearing on their marks.
  - **After the whistle** (`TickPostJog`): a beat to look around, then everyone on his feet jogs toward the dead ball (the offense gathering 7 yd behind it, the defense 5 yd in front, each keeping half his lateral spread), through the post-play, banner and play-call states; the pre-snap walk takes them on to the formation from wherever that got them. Men who are down, celebrating or holding the ball stay as the sim shows them.
  - **The ball in the hand**: seated on the palm between the fingers and the thumb (`GetBallHold` from `hand_r`, `middle_01_r`, `thumb_02_r`), not floating ahead of the wrist.
  - **Drop depth in the sim**: the only under-center pass play (`ace_curl`) already dropped 5 yd (6 deep); the play-action boots drop 3 behind the fake. The playbook's default drop from under center is now 5 yd for any play added later. Calibration unchanged.
  - **Timing gate** (`Tools/Anim/check_timing.py`): every clip family against real-game ranges (release and follow-through, three and five step drop durations and ground speeds, contact-to-ground, get-ups, catches, cuts, kicks, loops) and against the MaddenRef p10-p90 by category (one-shots the game chains are noted, not failed): 105 clips, 0 outside their real-world range.
  - Verified: `-TagGapTest` (throws at 22-38 s, drops and pocket at 51.5-61 s, `Saved/MoveTest/gap_drops.png`, `gap_throws2.png`), `-TagMoveTest` before/after sheets (`cmp3_r0.png`, `cmp3_r2.png`), import of 147 clips with an exact round trip, auto-watch games with 0 errors.
  - **Published reference points** the timing gate is built on (cited in `check_timing.py`): NFL time to throw 2.81 s (2024, PFF from Next Gen Stats); a release of 0.35-0.47 s with the front foot down ~0.21 s before the ball leaves (video analytics of NFL quarterbacks, USC Sport Science, Senior Bowl motion analysis); ball-carrier season-leading speeds of 22.4 mph (Next Gen Stats 2024-25; the sim's 99 speed is 22.5 mph); a five step drop ending ~7 yd deep (Human Kinetics). Against these: every throw's plant was 0.10 s before release and is now 0.20 (release and follow-through moved two frames; `THROW_RELEASE`, `ThrowKeyedStart` follow).
  - **The huddle** (`TickPostJog`): the offense forms a ring 7.5 yd behind the ball (1.3 m radius, the quarterback at the open side nearest the line facing his men), the defense a looser ring 5.5 yd in front; every man faces the middle when he arrives and breaks to the formation when the play is called.
  - **The umpire** (`TickUmpire`): a sixth official who works from the offensive backfield (9 yd behind the ball, off the quarterback's line), jogs to the dead ball at the whistle, carries it in his hand to the next spot (`Game::ballOn`, `ballY`), sets it on the turf nose downfield and returns to his position; off the field for kicks. The ball is drawn in his hand and then on the new spot until the next play's sim takes over.
  - **Found and fixed on the way:** `CallPlays` dropped the play's sim and hid every athlete before the next play was built, so the walk-in from the last play had nothing to walk from (the auto-watch log read "0 of 0 known men walk in") and the field stood empty through a user's play call. The men now stay on the field through the call (`Between`, director-driven), the huddle forms during it, and the next play's formation walks in from there: logs read "16 of 17 known men walk in, farthest 30 yd", the snap waits 4-6 s for the formation to set, and `tag_auto_setup.png` shows the line in its stances while the backs and the defense are still jogging to their spots.
  - **Running gait measured against sprint biomechanics** (`AAthlete::GaitStats`, logged per route in `-TagMoveTest`): foot touchdowns counted on the shown body while a man runs faster than 8 m/s give cadence and step length. Published sprint kinematics: 10.0 m/s at 4.70 steps/s is a 2.13 m step (Freelap / University of Oregon); elite maxima 5.06-5.28 steps/s at 10.6-10.8 m/s (Journal of Human Kinetics); field-sport athletes accelerating over 5-10 m: 4.04 steps/s, 1.66 m. Ours: the receiver at 9.4 m/s runs 4.82 steps/s with a 1.95 m step (the proportional reference at 9.4 m/s is ~4.6 steps/s, 2.04 m: cadence 4 % high, step 4 % short); the corner breaking at 8.5 m/s runs 4.28 steps/s, 1.98 m. That is the sped-up capture plus stride warping landing within a few percent of a real sprinter, measured rather than eyeballed.
  - **Stance heights against the 12-clip aggregates** (`check_timing.py` now gates them): the look targets put a three point stance's hips at 0.70-0.81 m and a shotgun quarterback's at 0.88-0.96; line coaching has the hips level with or above the shoulders. Ours had the three point hips at 0.57 (a sprinter's set, below the shoulders), the four point at 0.60 and the shotgun at 0.84. Re-keyed: three point 0.71 with the shoulders at 0.65 and the down hand on its fingertips (the rig's 0.49 m arm sets the reach), four point 0.72, shotgun 0.90; the snap catch and the get-off start from the new poses. All seven stances are now inside their bands.
  - **Acceleration against NFL Scouting Combine splits** (`check_timing.py`): the sim's acceleration law (vmax 8.6 + (speed - 50) × 0.049 yd/s, tau 1 - (accel - 50) × 0.0078 s, dv/dt = vmax/tau × max(0.12, 1 - v/vmax)), integrated from a standing start, puts a 90-speed / 85-accel receiver at 1.59 / 2.58 / 4.47 s over 10 / 20 / 40 yd and an average one (86 / 82) at 1.63 / 2.63 / 4.56, against the Combine's 1.55 (10 yd, 2026 receivers), ~2.59 (20 yd, drafted players) and 4.52 (40 yd, 848 receivers since 2003; 4.44 in 2026). Every start, lean and stride on the field follows this curve.
  - **Sprint cadence, step and body height, repeated runs.** The gait log is noisy run to run (motion matching picks different clips): at the 1.3× play-rate cap the receiver at 9.4 m/s measured 4.8, 5.3, 6.0 and 5.8 steps/s (steps 1.95-1.57 m); at 1.1× 5.1 and 5.5 steps/s (1.85, 1.70 m). The real sprinter's ~4.6 steps/s and 2.04 m say the capture sped up 1.3× is quick-footed, so the cap is now 1.1× and stride warping (which does not change cadence) covers the rest of the speed. Hips while sprinting 0.85-0.89 m and head 1.40-1.46 m against the sprint bands of 0.90-0.99 and 1.32-1.54; the stride warp's hip drop was eased (22 → 10 cm per unit of stretch) to keep the hips up.
  - **Side by side with game photographs** (`Saved/MoveTest/sbs_stance.png`, `sbs_throw.png`, `sbs_run.png`, `sbs_tackle.png`; the references are freely licensed Wikimedia Commons photographs, credits in `Saved/MoveTest/reference/LICENSES.txt`: a Chiefs guard in his three point stance, McNabb at release, Peterson carrying through traffic, a Navy back wrapped from behind). What differed and was changed: the real lineman's eyes are up at the man across and his off forearm rests on his thigh, where ours looked at the turf with both hands hanging (stance re-keyed with a flat back, hips 0.76 m, shoulders 0.82, head 0.79 against the aggregate's 0.70-0.81 / 0.77 medians, eyes across the line; the rig's 0.49 m arm cannot reach the turf from a flat back, so the football layer plants the down hand, both hands in a four point: `StanceHandAlpha`; `sbs_stance3.png` before / after); the real carrier runs with his pad level down where our sprint capture is upright (ball carriers now carry 7° of extra trunk lean through the football layer); the real carrier has both hands clamped on the ball through contact where ours kept one arm tucked (the five paired carrier clips now bring the off hand over the ball from the hit to the ground). The throw matched: upright, front leg braced, back toe down, arm at full extension, off hand out.
  - **Stride warping** (`ApplyStride`): above the 9.1 m/s the sped-up sprint capture covers, each foot's offset from the pelvis along the direction of travel is scaled by speed / 9.1 (at most 1.25) with the hips a touch lower and the legs following through IK, so a 10-11 m/s carrier lengthens his stride instead of skating. `-TagMoveTest`'s receiver now sprints at 10 m/s to exercise it.

### Phase 1. Reference targets (1 to 2 days)
